# Abstract

A decision-ready presentation reduces the audience's cost of understanding: it states a conclusion, organizes support, preserves evidence, and makes the next decision visible. “Authority” means disciplined usefulness and traceability, not prestige asserted by design. [INFERENCIA][source_ref:LEGACY-KB-41-PRESENTATIONS-V1]

</abstract>

# Routing

Use as a structural reference for executive decks, one-pagers, and reports. Active Content, Voice, and Visual canon govern implementation.

</routing>
<knowledge>

# Pattern

Open with a relevant tension or decision, use conclusion-bearing titles, organize up to three non-overlapping supports, bind evidence to the claim it supports, vary visual intensity intentionally, and close with a bounded decision or next movement. A slide is successful when the audience can identify its dominant claim, supporting evidence, and role in the narrative without presenter rescue. [METODOLOGIA]

</knowledge>
<evidence>

# Evidence

This pattern consolidates internal references; it is not empirical evidence of ROI, persuasion, retention, or executive authority.

</evidence>
<decisions>

# Decisions

Prefer an editable, accessible deck with speaker-note sources. Use Studio PDF as visual raw only when editability or accessibility is required.

</decisions>
<assumptions>

# Assumptions

[SUPUESTO] The presentation serves a real audience decision rather than acting only as documentation.

</assumptions>
<limits>

# Limits

Minto, MECE, transformation arcs, and “WOW” are tools, not mandatory labels or proof of quality.

</limits>
<edge_cases>

# Edge cases

If the audience needs exploration rather than a recommendation, state competing hypotheses. If evidence is confidential, provide a portable digest or approved citation—not a private locator.

</edge_cases>
<acceptance>

# Acceptance

The deck's argument is reconstructable from titles, each strong claim has an adjacent source or note, density supports actual-size reading, and the final movement matches the evidence.

</acceptance>
