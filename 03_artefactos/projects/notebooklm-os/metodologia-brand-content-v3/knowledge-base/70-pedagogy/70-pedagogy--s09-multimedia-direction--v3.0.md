# Abstract

S09 uses multimedia to condense complexity with medium-specific structure, hierarchy, contrast, and consistent identity. Producing more formats is not the goal; improving understanding, feedback, or action is. [METODOLOGIA][source_ref:NFC-DRV-S09]

# Prerequisites and concepts

Prerequisite: one grounded thesis, audience, and desired response. Concepts: content volume versus value; medium grammar; hierarchy, negative space, contrast; art direction as specification; negative prompts; reusable brand system; accessibility and rights.

# Sequence

Compare a saturated and a hierarchical artifact at actual size. Identify thesis, focus, reading/listening order, and visual function. Study references to extract rules, not copies. Compile two separate briefs for two media. Generate or prototype. Verify content, actual-size legibility/listenability, accessibility, rights, and identity.

# Misconceptions

One generic prompt fits every medium; a content calendar is a strategy; personal taste is universal; text over imagery is premium; tool names, aspect ratios, or style trends are permanent canon.

# Practice and transfer

Translate one thesis into two media with distinct structure, source sets, negative prompts, accessibility criteria, and acceptance. Explain what remains invariant and what changes. [PEDAGOGIA]

</knowledge>
<evidence>

# Evidence

Expected evidence: two briefs, source IDs, artifact/prototype, actual-size review, accessibility check, asset decision, and comparison rationale.

</evidence>
<decisions>

# Decisions

Preserve thesis and evidence; adapt interaction, pacing, composition, and accessibility to each medium. Rebuild corrupted generated text in an editable tool.

</decisions>
<assumptions>

# Assumptions

[SUPUESTO] At least one output or prototype can be inspected in its real consumption context.

</assumptions>
<limits>

# Limits

No claim of improved comprehension, reach, or retention follows without measurement. Reference access does not grant reproduction rights.

</limits>
<edge_cases>

# Edge cases

If a channel cannot meet accessibility, provide an equivalent companion. If a visual adds no information, use text. If generative tools cannot preserve brand assets, add them in postproduction.

</edge_cases>
<acceptance>

# Acceptance

The learner produces two genuinely medium-specific briefs that retain the same grounded thesis and meet format, identity, rights, and accessibility checks. [PEDAGOGIA]

</acceptance>
